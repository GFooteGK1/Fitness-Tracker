### Preparation and execution

Start each day with three minutes of easy cycling or walking, then prepare the
actual movements. For the first compound exercise, propose three rehearsal sets
of 8, 5 and 3 reps with progressively more relevant resistance. Target **RPE 3–4
for each preparation set**, keeping these easy and well short of fatigue; rest
60–90 seconds as needed. For the second compound,
propose two easy rehearsals of 6 and 3 reps with the same recovery principle. Use
one light five-rep rehearsal when setting up each later exercise. The Pallof
rehearsal is five per side. These counts and the 13-minute combined preparation
allowance are proposals; unfamiliar technique may need more instruction and time.

For every working exercise except Pallof presses, select a load
that permits the prescribed range at **RPE 7–8 (about 2–3 reps in reserve)** and controlled,
tolerable movement. No exact pounds, percent of 1RM or historical capacity is
inferred. Rehearsals help estimate the first working load. Record reps, load,
setup, actual set RPE and any symptoms after **every working set**. Intended RIR
does not substitute for reported RPE or prove achieved effort.

If the effort limit arrives below the lower rep target, stop, log what happened,
and reduce resistance for the next set. If the upper target is clearly easier
than intended, record that evidence and choose a better-matched load; do not
append unplanned sets or require failure. Avoid forcing repetitions through loss
of control or a reported symptom limitation. Clarify a limitation and revise the
affected work rather than assuming a different exercise is cleared.

For Pallof presses, target **RPE 6–7 for each side of each set**, using resistance
that allows quiet trunk control and normal breathing throughout. Stop or reduce resistance when rotation or bracing quality
breaks down; this is not a failure-seeking set. Ten is per side, not a combined
count. The two sets refer to two rounds of both sides.

Take the stated rest, and extend it if needed to maintain the next set's intended
effort and execution. Between exercises, recover and set up the next station
before starting. Rest is not shortened to force a time target. If time overruns,
omit a final accessory set first and record the change; if that cannot preserve
the remaining main work with recovery, report the time conflict and revise.

## Evidence and limits

The [2026 ACSM position stand](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)
supports individualizing resistance training to the goal and reports that greater
weekly volume can enhance hypertrophy; frequency differences were not meaningful
when volume was equated. Its authors discuss 2–3 RIR as a practical effort target,
while noting uncertainty about exact optimal effort thresholds. These group-level
findings do not validate this exact split, exercise list, starting dose or athlete.

Exercise and scheduling choices here are proposed coaching judgments. Equipment
alone does not establish superior stimulus; bodyweight options remain legitimate
when the athlete can receive the intended stimulus and progress them. A loaded
hinge is proposed because the goal calls for a scalable posterior-chain option,
not because bodyweight bridges are universally inappropriate.
